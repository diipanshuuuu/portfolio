    field notes

    Lifting Indian PII recall from 69% to 97% locally oct 2026

    Pure regex catches structured IDs like PAN and IFSC quickly, yet misses noisy Aadhaar scans, free-form addresses, and Indian names in multilingual PDFs. When we benchmarked our first rule-only pass at Hacktoberfest Hack Day Pune across 150 test documents, recall stalled at 68.9%.

    Pairing Verhoeff checksum rules with an on-device NER pass and Tesseract OCR across English, Hindi, and Marathi lifted recall to 97.3% at 100% precision in ~60 ms on CPU. Every token stays inside the local FastAPI sidecar before the masked prompt reaches ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude, with an optional Qwen3-4B deep scan through Ollama for ambiguous paragraphs.

    Rewriting SysCleanX from Python to a 6 MB Rust binary 2025 — 2026

    SysCleanX started as a Python utility packed into a heavy executable that took seconds just to unpack its runtime. Rewriting it in Rust with egui and direct Win32 calls shrunk it into a single ~6 MB native app that starts cold in milliseconds with no background service and no telemetry.

    The hardest part wasn't multithreaded scanning across 81 browser profiles and caches—it was making cleanup boringly safe. Every candidate path passes through a hard-coded safety guard that rejects system roots, refuses to follow symlinks or junction points, and keeps startup toggles reversible.
